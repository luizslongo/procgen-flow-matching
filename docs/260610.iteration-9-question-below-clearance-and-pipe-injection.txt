OBJECTIVE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

ASCII inspection of the Iteration 8 Overworld output revealed two
remaining problems:

   Many chunks had QuestionFull tiles with a Solid (or Breakable)
   tile directly below them. Mario hits ? blocks by jumping into
   them from below; a Solid tile at (x, y+1) blocks the head-impact
   path and the block cannot be triggered. The Iteration 7
   IsBuriedInSolid rule (at least 2 of 4 cardinal neighbors are
   Empty) passed these configurations because the LEFT, RIGHT, and
   ABOVE neighbors were often Empty even when the BELOW was Solid.

   Six of ten Overworld chunks contained zero pipe tiles in the
   raw model output. The Iteration 8 MinCompletePerChunk override
   in PipeSupportRepair fires only on chunks where the model
   placed at least one PipeTopLeft. Chunks with no model-placed
   pipe tiles cannot benefit from the override because the
   pipeline has no anchor to extend or remove.

Iteration 9 fixes both with a targeted QuestionFull rule and a new
structural-augmentation pass.

PART 1: DESIGN - TWO REFINEMENTS
================================================================================

1.1 QUESTIONFULL "EMPTY DIRECTLY BELOW" RULE

The Iteration 7 QuestionBlockRepair already enforced four validity
rules:

   (R1) Not on the bottom row
   (R2) At least 2 of 4 cardinal neighbors are Empty
   (R3) A Solid or Breakable tile exists within 8 rows below
   (R4) Not adjacent to a pipe or bullet bill structure

Iteration 9 adds a fifth rule:

   (R5) The cell directly below (x, y+1) MUST be Empty

R5 is more specific than R2. R2 counts ANY 2 of 4 neighbors as Empty
and passes configurations where LEFT, RIGHT, and ABOVE are Empty but
BELOW is Solid. R5 specifically requires the BELOW cell to be Empty
because that is the direction from which Mario approaches a ? block;
his head impacts the block from (x, y+1) upward into (x, y).

R3 is updated to start its ground-search at y+2 instead of y+1
because y+1 is now constrained to be Empty by R5; a ground tile at
y+1 would violate R5 and therefore is not a legal way to satisfy
the support condition.

1.2 PIPE INJECTION

A new PipeInjection class runs as the LAST pass in the iteration
loop, after all model-driven structural validations. It synthesizes
2-tile-tall pipes at ground-level columns when the chunk has fewer
complete pipes than a randomly-sampled target.

Target sampling: uniform random in [MinCompletePerChunk,
MaxCompletePerChunk] (default [1, 3]) per chunk per iteration. The
random source is the same seeded Random used by the rest of the
repair pipeline for reproducibility.

Placement constraints for each injection attempt:

   The bottom row at (x, groundY) and (x+1, groundY) must be Solid
   or Breakable to support the pipe.

   The pipe area at columns x and x+1, rows pipeTopY through
   groundY-1, must be all Empty so the injection does not
   overwrite existing tiles.

   The cell directly above the pipe cap at (x, pipeTopY-1) and
   (x+1, pipeTopY-1) must be Empty so the injection does not
   create a pipe blocked from above (mirrors the PipeTopClearance
   constraint).

The pass scans columns left to right and injects at the first
column that satisfies all three constraints. When no column is
suitable, the pass stops and leaves the chunk at fewer pipes than
the target. This is the graceful failure mode for chunks crowded
with other structures and for Treetop chunks (which have no
continuous ground for the pipe to anchor on).

Injected pipe height is fixed at 2 tiles: 1 top row plus 1 body
row. This is the minimum complete pipe and the most common short
pipe in canonical SMB content.

2.1 UPDATED: QuestionBlockRepair.cs

Added HasEmptyDirectlyBelow private method called from
IsValidQuestionFullPlacement. Updated HasGroundBelow loop to start

2.2 NEW: PipeInjection.cs (137 lines)

Public entry point:

   public static bool RepairOnce(TileMap chunk,
       ChunkStructureRepairConfig config, Random rng)

The implementation samples the target pipe count, calls
StructureCounter.CountCompletePipes to get the current count, and
loops calling a private TryInjectOnePipe until the target is met
or no column is suitable.

TryInjectOnePipe scans columns left to right, validates the
placement constraints via CanInjectAt, and writes the four pipe
tiles (PipeTopLeft, PipeTopRight, PipeBodyLeft, PipeBodyRight)
into the chunk on the first match.

PART 3: ORCHESTRATOR UPDATE
================================================================================

ChunkStructureRepair.RepairAll now invokes 11 passes per iteration:

   (1) BottomRowCompletion
   (2) CeilingCompletion
   (3) PipeRepair
   (4) PipeSupportRepair
   (5) PipeTopClearance
   (6) BulletBillRepair
   (7) BulletBillSupportRepair
   (8) BulletBillLauncherClearance
   (9) EnemyRepair
   (10) QuestionBlockRepair
   (11) PipeInjection

PipeInjection runs LAST so it sees the final supported-pipe total
after all model-driven validation has settled. Any pipes the model
placed (and that survived the validation) are counted; PipeInjection
only adds new pipes when the count is below the random target.

The loop continues to iterate after PipeInjection runs so the next
iteration's PipeRepair, PipeSupportRepair, PipeTopClearance see the
newly-injected pipe and confirm it as a valid complete supported
structure with cleared cap. Convergence is typically reached in 2
to 3 iterations.

PART 6: HONEST DISCUSSION OF PIPE INJECTION FOR THESIS
================================================================================

6.1 PIPE INJECTION CROSSES A METHODOLOGICAL LINE

The repair passes added in Iterations 5 through 8 are all
post-processing: they validate and adjust tiles the model placed.
PipeInjection adds new tiles the model did not predict at all.
This is a structural augmentation, not a repair.

The thesis writeup should be explicit about this. Iteration 9
moves the pipeline from "validate model output" to "validate +
augment to guarantee minimum density of biome-defining structures".

6.2 DEFENSIBLE FRAMING

The contribution of the CFM model is the tile-type distribution and
the spatial pattern at the chunk level: where pipes appear, where
question blocks cluster, where biomes vary. The contribution of the
repair pipeline is the structural-integrity invariants the
per-tile MSE loss provably cannot enforce. PipeInjection extends
this to a structural DENSITY invariant: every Overworld chunk should
have at least one pipe to match the biome's canonical level design.

The injected pipes are placed in legal positions (on ground, with
clearance, away from other structures), are short and visually
canonical, and respect the [1, 3] per-chunk count target that
matches real SMB Overworld levels.

6.3 CONTRAST WITH WAVE FUNCTION COLLAPSE

Wave Function Collapse achieves valid output by propagating
explicitly-specified constraints over the whole grid. The
CFM-plus-repair pipeline learns soft constraints from data (the
CFM model) and enforces hard structural rules in post-processing
(the repair pipeline). The two approaches converge in the limit:
both produce chunks that satisfy the structural rules. They
differ in how the rules are specified (learned vs. explicit) and
in how the generation samples the space of valid chunks (continuous
flow vs. constraint propagation).

The PipeInjection pass is the moment in our pipeline where the
"learned" side admits its limits and falls back to an explicit
rule. The thesis is honest about this fallback.

6.4 RANDOMNESS REPRODUCIBILITY

Pipe injection uses the same seeded Random as the rest of the
repair pipeline. Reviewers can reproduce exact output by running
with the same seed.
