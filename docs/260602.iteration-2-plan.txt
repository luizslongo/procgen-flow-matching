OBJECTIVE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Iteration 1 produced a mechanically complete pipeline: VGLC data loader,
U-Net architecture, Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) training loop, Euler
ODE solver for inference, FailureModeAnalyzer for evaluation, and a PNG
renderer with sprites extracted from the VGLC level images. The pipeline
trained successfully on CPU (2k steps, loss 1.08 -> 0.14) and on RTX 5060
GPU (20k steps, loss 1.07 -> 0.028).

The qualitative output of Iteration 1 has known limitations: generated
chunks exhibit discontinuous ground rows, broken pipe structures,
floating enemies that are not supported by tiles below them, and almost
no structurally-valid bullet bill cannons. A 5x reduction in the CFM
loss (from the 2k-step CPU baseline to the 20k-step GPU run) did not
produce a proportional improvement in the spatial coherence of the
generated samples.

Iteration 2 targets these qualitative limitations. Each planned
experiment in this document corresponds to one specific hypothesis about
why Iteration 1 underperforms qualitatively, with a planned change and
a measurable success criterion.

PART 1: LIMITATIONS OBSERVED IN ITERATION 1
================================================================================

The Iteration 1 20k-step model exhibits four distinct failure patterns
when generating 10 chunks at NumSteps (NFE) = 50.

1.1 DISCONTINUOUS GROUND

    The bottom row of the generated chunk is intended to be continuous
    Solid (X) tiles. The model produces ground rows with 4-10 gaps per
    chunk on average. This is the dominant failure mode, accounting for
    62% of all FailureModeAnalyzer violations in the 20k-step run.

1.2 BROKEN PIPE STRUCTURES

    Pipes in the VGLC encoding are 2 tiles wide and 2+ tiles tall:
    PipeTopLeft (<) + PipeTopRight (>) on the top row, PipeBodyLeft ([)
    + PipeBodyRight (]) on the body rows. The model frequently produces
    isolated < tiles without a matching >, or [ tiles without a top, or
    horizontally-flipped arrangements (> on the left, < on the right).
    The model has learned that pipe tiles exist but has not learned the
    structural constraint that pipe parts must form a contiguous,
    correctly-oriented 2-wide column.

1.3 FLOATING ENEMIES

    The Enemy (E) tile represents a Goomba walking on a surface. The
    canonical placement in the training data is on top of a Solid (X)
    tile. The model frequently emits E tiles that float in the sky with
    no Solid tile immediately below. This is the FloatingEnemy failure
    mode.

1.4 ABSENT OR FRAGMENTED BULLET BILLS

    BulletBillLauncher (B) and BulletBillBody (b) account for only 29
    tiles out of 41,132 in the entire training corpus, a frequency of
    0.07%. They appear in only 2 levels out of 32 (mario-5-1 with 6
    tiles, mario-7-1 with 23 tiles). The 20k-step model produces B and
    b tiles at approximately the right marginal frequency but rarely
    composes them into a valid B-on-top-of-b cannon structure.
    Aggregate BrokenBulletBill violations: 11 across 10 chunks.

PART 2: EXPERIMENT A - LARGER MODEL CAPACITY
================================================================================

2.1 HYPOTHESIS

    The U-Net architecture used in Iteration 1 has BaseChannels = 64.
    The total parameter count is approximately 3 million. This may be
    insufficient to encode the long-range spatial dependencies required
    to produce continuous ground rows of 28 tiles or correctly-stacked
    multi-tile pipe structures.

2.2 EMPIRICAL FINDING (2026-06-02)

    Attempted to increase BaseChannels from 64 to 256, then to 128,
    keeping all other hyperparameters (LearningRate 5e-5, BatchSize 32,
    NumSteps 20000, manual seed 42, gradient clip max_norm 0.5) fixed.

    Both attempts produced training loss equal to positive infinity at
    step 0, with NaN at all subsequent steps. Step 0 is computed BEFORE
    any optimizer update, so this is a forward-pass numerical issue
    caused by the initial random weights, not a gradient explosion that
    a smaller learning rate or more aggressive gradient clipping could
    fix.

    Root cause analysis: the UnetBaseline architecture in
    c2-pcg.flow-matching-model places GroupNorm layers inside the
    ResidualConvBlock hidden path but adds an UN-normalized shortcut
    (Identity or 1x1 Conv) to the result. Across 5 residual blocks,
    the activation variance compounds. At BaseChannels = 64 the
    compounded variance happens to stay within the FP32 representable
    range; at BaseChannels = 128 or higher it overflows on the first
    forward pass with the seed-42 weight initialization.

2.3 PROPOSED ARCHITECTURAL FIX

    To enable BaseChannels > 64 stably, the ResidualConvBlock needs
    one of the following changes:

      Option 1: Zero-initialize the SecondConv weights of every
        ResidualConvBlock. At init each block computes result = 0 +
        shortcut = identity. Variance does not compound. This is the
        canonical fix used by Stable Diffusion, FLUX, and other large
        U-Net based generative models. Effort: approximately 5 lines
        of code in ResidualConvBlock.cs plus optional fix to other
        blocks. No change to forward-pass logic.

      Option 2: Add a normalization layer on the shortcut path. The
        shortcut becomes Norm(Shortcut(x)) instead of Shortcut(x).
        Stops variance compounding by normalizing every shortcut
        addition. Effort: approximately 10 lines.

      Option 3: Scale the residual addition by 1/sqrt(2). The
        forward returns hidden / sqrt(2) + shortcut / sqrt(2). Keeps
        the variance at 1.0 instead of doubling per block. Effort:
        approximately 3 lines.

    Recommended option for Iteration 2: Option 1 (zero-init
    SecondConv). Standard pattern, minimal code change, no behavioral
    surprises at convergence time.

2.4 PLANNED CHANGE (DEFERRED)

    Once the architectural fix is implemented, increase BaseChannels
    to 128 first. Verify training is stable. Then attempt 256.

    Field changes (after architectural fix):
      ResidualConvBlock.cs: zero-init SecondConv
      PcgFlowMatchingTrainEntryPoint.cs:    BaseChannels = 128 then 256
      PcgFlowMatchingGenerateEntryPoint.cs: same

    BatchSize remains 32.

2.5 STATUS

    BLOCKED on architectural fix. Reverted BaseChannels to 64 on the
    iteration-2-bigger-model branch on 2026-06-02 to unblock other
    experiments. Re-priority: Experiment B (Part 3) is now Priority 1.

PART 3: EXPERIMENT B - LONG-TAIL TILE TYPES VIA CLASS-BALANCED LOSS
================================================================================

3.1 HYPOTHESIS

    The current CfmLossComputer computes a Mean Squared Error (MSE)
    between predicted and target velocity tensors over all 14 one-hot
    channels and all 28 by 14 spatial positions. Because rare tile
    types (BulletBillLauncher, BulletBillBody) account for 0.07% of
    training pixels, the per-pixel loss provides almost no gradient
    signal for correctly modeling rare tiles. The model is effectively
    rewarded the same for ignoring rare tile types as for producing
    them correctly.

3.2 PLANNED CHANGE

    Modify CfmLossComputer.ComputeLoss to weight the per-pixel MSE
    contribution by the inverse frequency of the target tile type.

    Concrete implementation sketch:
      1. Precompute tile frequency array F[TileType] from the training
         set. Bullet bills have F approximately 0.0007; Solid has F
         approximately 0.0875.
      2. Compute inverse-frequency weights W[TileType] = 1 / F[TileType]
         normalized so that the average weight is 1.
      3. In ComputeLoss, take the argmax of x1 (the one-hot target) to
         get the tile type at each position. Multiply the squared error
         contribution at that position by W[tile_type_at_position].
      4. Reduce by mean as before.

    This is a small change to one file (CfmLossComputer.cs) and one
    new precomputed file (tile-frequencies.csv) extracted from the
    VGLC training data.

3.3 MEASURABLE OUTCOME

    BulletBill-related violations should drop from the current 11 per
    10 chunks to under 4 per 10 chunks. Other rare-tile failure modes
    (FloatingEnemy in particular) may also improve.

3.4 EFFORT AND TIMING

    Code change: approximately 30 lines in CfmLossComputer plus a small
    helper to compute tile frequencies.
    Training time: same as Experiment A (model size unchanged).
    Total elapsed wall-clock: under 2 hours including precomputation
    and verification.

PART 4: EXPERIMENT C - DISCRETE FLOW MATCHING FOR CATEGORICAL DATA
================================================================================

4.1 HYPOTHESIS

    Tile maps are inherently categorical: each position is exactly one
    of 14 discrete tile types. Iteration 1 encodes this as a one-hot
    tensor and applies continuous CFM, then takes argmax at inference
    time to discretize. This is a known suboptimal pairing of objective
    and data type. Mixtures of tile types are valid intermediate states
    during the Euler integration but have no physical meaning in the
    tile-map domain.

    Discrete Flow Matching (Campbell et al., 2024) is a recently
    published variant of flow matching designed specifically for
    categorical data. It replaces the continuous interpolation path
    with a probabilistic mixture along the categorical simplex, and
    replaces the MSE objective with a categorical cross-entropy
    objective.

4.2 PLANNED CHANGE

    Replace CfmLossComputer with a new DiscreteCfmLossComputer that
    implements the Campbell et al. (2024) objective. Replace
    EulerOdeSolver with a categorical sampling procedure that
    integrates the discrete-time Markov chain implied by the trained
    velocity field.

    Reference: Campbell et al., "Generative Flows on Discrete State
    Spaces: Enabling Multimodal Flows with Applications to Protein
    Co-Design," 2024.

    This is the most ambitious experiment in the Iteration 2 plan and
    is documented as an option rather than a commitment.

4.3 MEASURABLE OUTCOME

    All four failure modes (DiscontinuousGround, BrokenPipe*,
    FloatingEnemy, BrokenBulletBill) should show improvement because
    the model is now optimized over the correct objective for the data
    type. The per-chunk total violation count should drop substantially
    from the current 8.2 mean.

4.4 EFFORT AND TIMING

    Code change: approximately 200 to 400 lines spread across
    CfmLossComputer, EulerOdeSolver, and possibly the U-Net output
    head. Includes implementing and verifying the discrete sampling
    procedure.
    Training time: similar to Iteration 1.
    Total elapsed wall-clock: 2 to 3 days of focused work, including
    reading the paper, implementation, debugging, and verification.

PART 5: EXPERIMENT D - CONDITIONAL GENERATION (TCC OBJECTIVE O4)
================================================================================

5.1 HYPOTHESIS

    The original TCC objective O4 specifies that the model should
    accept a conditional input describing the desired biome or level
    type (overworld, underground, treetop, castle, etc.) and produce
    a chunk matching that condition. Iteration 1 generates from
    unconditional noise; the output biome is not controllable.

5.2 PLANNED CHANGE

    Add a conditional embedding vector (length to be determined; a
    reasonable default is 64) to the U-Net forward path. The
    conditional vector is concatenated with the time embedding before
    being injected into each ResidualConvBlock.

    The conditional label is derived from the VGLC level name during
    training (mario-1-1 -> overworld, mario-1-2 -> underground, etc.)
    and provided as input at inference time.

    For TCC scope, support 4 biome labels:
      0 = overworld
      1 = underground
      2 = treetop
      3 = castle

5.3 MEASURABLE OUTCOME

    Generated samples for a given biome label should visually match
    the expected biome's tile statistics. A treetop chunk should
    contain elevated platforms; an underground chunk should contain
    pipes and few open-sky regions; a castle chunk should contain
    BulletBill structures.

    A quantitative biome-classifier metric can be added to
    FailureModeAnalyzer, comparing the predicted biome of a generated
    chunk to its requested biome label.

5.4 EFFORT AND TIMING

    Code change: U-Net forward signature change, embedding lookup
    addition, training loop modification to pass labels. Approximately
    100 lines.
    Training time: similar to Iteration 1.
    Total elapsed wall-clock: 1 to 2 days of focused work.

PART 7: SUCCESS CRITERIA AND EVALUATION PROTOCOL
================================================================================

7.1 PER-EXPERIMENT REPORTING

    Every completed experiment produces a paired result report in
    docs/ named YYMMDD.iteration-2-exp-X-result.txt, where X is the
    experiment letter and YYMMDD is the completion date. Each report
    contains:
      - Configuration: hyperparameters used
      - Loss trajectory: initial, final, plateau step
      - 10 generated chunks: ASCII + PNG renderings
      - FailureModeAnalyzer aggregate counts
      - Comparison against the Iteration 1 baseline
      - Qualitative observations
      - Conclusion: does this experiment proceed to manuscript, or
        was it superseded by a later experiment

7.2 ITERATION 2 OVERALL SUCCESS CRITERION

    Iteration 2 is complete when the best-configured model produces
    chunks with the following aggregate metrics over 10 generated
    samples at NumSteps = 50, fixed seed:

      Mean total violations per chunk:  under 4.0
        (Iteration 1 baseline: 8.2)
      DiscontinuousGround per chunk:    under 2.0
        (Iteration 1 baseline: 5.1)
      BrokenPipe (any type) per chunk:  under 1.5
        (Iteration 1 baseline: 2.0)

    These targets are derived from the Iteration 1 measurements and
    represent a 50% reduction in dominant failure modes.
