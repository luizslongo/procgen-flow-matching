Iteration 5 implements a deterministic post-generation repair pass that
enforces hard structural constraints on the model's output. The thesis
framing positions this as a hybrid system: the Conditional Flow Matching
model learns SOFT constraints (tile-type distributions, spatial
patterns) from training data, and the repair pass enforces HARD
constraints (a pipe must be a complete 2-by-N rectangle, a bullet bill
must be a complete 1-by-N column, a QuestionFull must follow SMB
placement conventions) derived from explicit game-design rules.

The target violation rate after repair is approximately 1 to 3 per
chunk, which is below the original Iteration 1 baseline of 7.7 and
within reach of the user-specified "around 10" goal stated on 2026-06-09.

PART 1: DESIGN - VALIDITY RULES AND COMPLETE-OR-REMOVE POLICY
================================================================================

1.1 STRUCTURAL VALIDITY RULES

A tile of a structural type is VALID if and only if it participates in a
complete canonical instance of that structure. The canonical structures
are:

   Complete pipe: a 2-tile-wide column at least 2 tiles tall.
      Top row:    PipeTopLeft       PipeTopRight
      Body row n: PipeBodyLeft      PipeBodyRight   (n >= 1 rows)

   Complete bullet bill: a 1-tile-wide column at least 2 tiles tall.
      Top:        BulletBillLauncher
      Body row n: BulletBillBody                    (n >= 1 rows)

   QuestionFull (single-tile structure): valid placement is
      Not on the bottom row of the chunk.
      At least 2 of 4 cardinal neighbors are Empty (not buried).
      A Solid or Breakable tile exists within 8 rows below (ground
      reachable below).
      No cardinal neighbor is a pipe tile or bullet bill tile.

1.2 LOOSE-TILE DETECTION

A tile is LOOSE when it has a structural type but does not satisfy the
validity rule above. The FailureModeAnalyzer at
c2-pcg.flow-matching-eval/FailureModeAnalyzer.cs already detects four
specific looseness conditions (BrokenPipeHorizontal,
BrokenPipeTopLeft, BrokenPipeTopRight, BrokenBulletBill). The
ChunkStructureRepair generalization in Iteration 5 covers ALL eight
constraint violations implied by Section 1.1, including the four the
analyzer does not currently check (PipeTopRight without TopLeft to the
left, PipeBodyRight without TopRight or BodyRight above, etc.).

1.3 THE COMPLETE-OR-REMOVE POLICY

For each loose tile of a multi-tile structure (pipes, bullet bills),
two repair options are available:

   COMPLETE: write the missing pieces of the canonical structure
   anchored on the loose tile, so that the structure becomes valid.

   REMOVE: replace the loose tile with a neutral tile from
   SelectReplacementTile (Empty for positions higher than chunk.Height
   minus 3, Breakable for positions within 3 rows of the bottom).

The choice between COMPLETE and REMOVE depends on how many complete
structures of the same kind already exist in the chunk. The target
range of complete structures per chunk is [MinCompletePerChunk = 1,
MaxCompletePerChunk = 3]:

   existingCount == 0: always COMPLETE (chunk needs at least one)
   existingCount >= 3: always REMOVE (chunk has enough already)
   existingCount in {1, 2}: coin flip via seeded Random

The coin flip uses System.Random seeded with ChunkStructureRepairConfig.
RandomSeed (default 42, matching the training seed for reproducibility).
This biases the repaired chunk distribution toward [1, 3] complete
structures of each kind per chunk.

COMPLETE may fail even when chosen, if the surrounding cells needed to
form the canonical structure already contain other structural tiles
(not Empty, not Solid, not the desired pipe or bullet bill tile). In
that case, the repair falls through to REMOVE to avoid clobbering one
structure when trying to repair another.

QuestionFull tiles do not use the COMPLETE-or-REMOVE policy because they
are single-tile structures; misplaced blocks are simply removed via
SelectReplacementTile.

1.4 REPLACEMENT TILE SELECTION

SelectReplacementTile in StructureCounter returns:

   Breakable if y >= chunk.Height - 3 (within 3 rows of the bottom)
   Empty otherwise

The Breakable rule reflects the SMB convention that brick blocks at
jump-height above the ground are the canonical "single decorative
tile" placement. Replacing a loose pipe piece at y = chunk.Height - 2
with Empty would leave a one-tile gap in what should be a
ground-or-near-ground tile; replacing with Breakable preserves
structural density and matches the SMB aesthetic. Higher positions
become Empty (sky) because brick blocks at high altitudes are atypical.

1.5 ITERATIVE CONVERGENCE

The repair loop in ChunkStructureRepair.RepairAll runs each repair pass
(PipeRepair, BulletBillRepair, QuestionBlockRepair) in sequence and
repeats the sweep until a full iteration makes no changes. Iteration
is necessary because:

   Removing a buried QuestionFull may expose a PipeTopLeft whose
   completion target was previously blocked, enabling COMPLETE in the
   next iteration.

   Completing a pipe may turn what was an orphan PipeBodyLeft (no
   TopLeft above) into a valid body in a freshly completed pipe.

   Other compound effects.

The loop terminates when convergence is reached or after
MaxIterations = 10 (safety cap). Typical chunks converge in 2 to 3
iterations.

PART 2: IMPLEMENTATION - CLASS-BY-CLASS BREAKDOWN
================================================================================

2.1 ASSEMBLY LOCATION AND FILE LIST

All new classes live in the assembly c2-pcg.flow-matching-eval. Files
created in Iteration 5:

   ChunkStructureRepairConfig.cs       (34 lines)
   StructureCounter.cs                 (122 lines)
   PipeRepair.cs                       (214 lines)
   BulletBillRepair.cs                 (139 lines)
   QuestionBlockRepair.cs              (146 lines)
   ChunkStructureRepair.cs             (57 lines)

Total Iteration 5 code added to the eval assembly: approximately 712
lines of C#, plus 96 lines of integration code in the generate
entrypoint (Part 3).

2.2 ChunkStructureRepairConfig.cs

State-only class containing tunable parameters as public fields:
MinCompletePerChunk = 1, MaxCompletePerChunk = 3, RandomSeed = 42,
MaxIterations = 10. Follows the project's state-action separation
convention.

2.3 StructureCounter.cs

Action class providing the count primitives and safe tile accessors
shared by all repair classes:

   CountCompletePipes(chunk)         - counts 2-by-N canonical pipes
   CountCompleteBulletBills(chunk)   - counts 1-by-N canonical bullets
   GetTile(chunk, x, y)              - bounds-checked tile read,
                                        returns TileTypeEnum.Error for
                                        out-of-bounds positions
   SetTile(chunk, x, y, tile)        - bounds-checked tile write,
                                        silently ignores oob
   IsPipeTile(tile)                  - membership predicate
   IsBulletBillTile(tile)            - membership predicate
   SelectReplacementTile(y, height)  - Breakable vs Empty by y

2.4 PipeRepair.cs

Two-pass repair. Pass 1 anchors each PipeTopLeft and attempts to
COMPLETE the canonical 2x2 pipe template at (x, y); Pass 2 removes
orphan PipeTopRight, PipeBodyLeft, and PipeBodyRight tiles that
cannot participate in a TopLeft-anchored structure. The
COMPLETE-vs-REMOVE decision uses
ChunkStructureRepairConfig.MinCompletePerChunk and MaxCompletePerChunk
to bias the count toward [1, 3] complete pipes per chunk.

2.5 BulletBillRepair.cs

Two-pass repair. Pass 1 processes each BulletBillLauncher and ensures
BulletBillBody at the position directly below; Pass 2 removes orphan
BulletBillBody tiles whose position above is neither launcher nor
another body. No upward launcher growth is attempted because adding
launchers where the model did not predict them is more aggressive than
the rest of the repair policy supports.

2.6 QuestionBlockRepair.cs

Single-pass repair applying the four SMB placement validity rules of
Section 1.1. Tiles failing any rule are replaced via
SelectReplacementTile. The 8-row ground-search radius (Rule 3) is a
generous approximation of the chunk-height distance over which an
Overworld block is still considered "reachable" without an explicit
playability analysis.

2.7 ChunkStructureRepair.cs

Orchestrator. The public entry point RepairAll(chunk, config) creates
a deep copy of the input chunk so the original is never mutated
(state-action separation: pure function semantics). The Random
instance is seeded once per chunk by config.RandomSeed for
reproducibility. The iterative loop runs PipeRepair, BulletBillRepair,
and QuestionBlockRepair in sequence and terminates on no-change or at
MaxIterations.

PART 3: INTEGRATION INTO THE GENERATE ENTRYPOINT
================================================================================

3.1 INTEGRATION POINT

ChunkStructureRepair runs in PcgFlowMatchingGenerateEntryPoint.cs
between MapGenerator.Generate (raw chunk production) and
FailureModeAnalyzer.Analyze (violation counting). The integration also
analyses each chunk TWICE - once on the raw model output and once on
the repaired output - so the impact of repair is visible per chunk and
in the aggregate without requiring an external A/B harness.

3.2 NEW CLI FLAG

The flag --no-repair disables the repair pass; when set, the
post-repair analysis operates on the unmodified chunk and the post
counts equal the pre counts. This enables explicit A/B comparison runs
("with repair" vs "without repair") without modifying code or
maintaining two separate generate binaries.

Default behavior is repair ENABLED.

3.3 OUTPUT FORMAT

Per-chunk output now reports two violation blocks:

   Violations (pre-repair):  <total>
     BrokenPipeHorizontal: ...
     BrokenPipeTopLeft:    ...
     BrokenPipeTopRight:   ...
     BrokenBulletBill:     ...
     FloatingEnemy:        ...
     DiscontinuousGround:  ...
   Violations (post-repair): <total>
     BrokenPipeHorizontal: ...
     (same breakdown)

Aggregate output similarly reports two blocks: pre-repair and
post-repair totals over the run.

PART 6: LIMITATIONS AND HONEST DISCUSSION FOR THESIS
================================================================================

6.1 SCOPE OF THE REPAIR

ChunkStructureRepair targets only three failure categories: pipes,
bullet bills, and QuestionFull placement. It does NOT address:

   DiscontinuousGround (the bottom-row continuity failure). This
   is the dominant failure mode in current Iteration 4 output (48
   percent of violations at step 10500). A separate
   BottomRowCompletion pass that forces the bottom row to Solid
   would fix this; it is intentionally not part of Iteration 5
   because the user-driven design discussion that produced this
   iteration focused on loose-tile structures, not row-level
   ground completion.

   FloatingEnemy (Goombas without a Solid below). A separate
   EnemySnapToSurface pass would fix this.

The expected violation reduction from Iteration 5 alone is therefore
bounded by the share of violations attributable to pipes, bullet
bills, and misplaced QuestionFull tiles. Based on the Iteration 4
step-10500 breakdown that is approximately 138 minus 66 minus 14 =
58 of the 138 total, or roughly 42 percent. After Iteration 5 the
expected remaining violations are dominated by DiscontinuousGround
and FloatingEnemy.

6.2 HONEST FRAMING FOR THESIS DEFENSE

The repair pass is explicitly a hard-rule post-processor. It does
NOT change the trained model, the training data, or the inference
procedure. The thesis should frame Iteration 5 as a HYBRID system:

   "The Conditional Flow Matching model learns soft structural
    constraints from training data and produces chunks that satisfy
    these constraints approximately. A subsequent deterministic
    repair pass enforces hard structural constraints derived from
    explicit game-design rules. The contribution of this thesis is
    the joint pipeline: data-driven generation followed by
    rule-based structural enforcement."

This framing acknowledges the hybrid nature honestly and aligns with
production game procedural generation pipelines, which almost always
combine learned generators with explicit post-processing.

6.3 EFFECT ON THE LEARNED-CONSTRAINTS CLAIM

A reviewer may ask whether the repair pass undermines the thesis
claim that CFM learns constraints from data. The honest answer is:
the repair enforces a tiny number of hard rules that the model is
provably unable to learn perfectly with the current 2518-chunk
training set and per-tile MSE loss (see docs/260609 Part 5 on the
five sources of irreducible violation rate). The repair handles
the gap between approximate learned constraints and absolute
constraint satisfaction. The CFM contribution is the soft constraint
learning; the repair is the hard constraint enforcement layer.

6.4 RANDOMNESS REPRODUCIBILITY

The coin flip in DecideCompleteOrRemove uses a Random seeded at
config.RandomSeed (42 by default). This produces deterministic
output across repeated runs with the same input chunk and seed.
Reviewers requesting exact reproduction of figures can re-run with
the same seed and expect bit-identical chunks.
