PART 1: DESIGN - SUPPORT VALIDATION AND POLICIES
================================================================================

1.1 STRUCTURAL SUPPORT RULE

A complete structural element (pipe, bullet bill, enemy) is VALID if
and only if it satisfies the support condition:

   Pipe at (x, y) to (x+1, bottomY): both (x, bottomY+1) and
   (x+1, bottomY+1) are Solid or Breakable, or bottomY == chunk.Height-1
   (implicit ground below the chunk).

   Bullet bill at (x, y) to (x, bottomY): (x, bottomY+1) is Solid or
   Breakable, or bottomY == chunk.Height-1.

   Enemy at (x, y): (x, y+1) is Solid or Breakable, or y == chunk.Height-1.

The implicit-ground rule recognizes that the chunk has a notional
ground level immediately below its bottom row. A structure that touches
the bottom row is considered supported even though the actual support
tile is outside the chunk.

1.2 EXTEND-OR-REMOVE POLICY FOR FLOATING MULTI-TILE STRUCTURES

Floating pipes and bullet bills are handled by an EXTEND-or-REMOVE
policy that mirrors the COMPLETE-or-REMOVE policy of Iteration 5,
operating on the count of already-supported structures of the same
kind in the chunk:

   existingSupportedCount < MinCompletePerChunk: EXTEND
   existingSupportedCount >= MaxCompletePerChunk: REMOVE
   in between: seeded coin flip

EXTEND grows the structure body downward through Empty cells until
reaching ground (Solid or Breakable below) or the chunk bottom
(implicit ground). REMOVE clears every tile in the structure column
via SelectReplacementTile.

EXTEND may fail when the downward path is blocked by another
structural tile (a Goomba, a Coin, another pipe). In that case the
action falls through to REMOVE to avoid clobbering one structure when
trying to ground another.

1.3 SNAP-OR-REMOVE POLICY FOR FLOATING ENEMIES

Floating enemies are handled by a SNAP-or-REMOVE policy:

   SNAP: search downward from the enemy for the nearest Solid or
   Breakable within 3 rows. If found at row k, and the intervening
   cells are Empty, move the enemy to (x, k-1) and clear the
   original cell via SelectReplacementTile.

   REMOVE: if no ground tile is within the 3-row search depth, or
   if the path to ground is blocked by another structural tile,
   clear the enemy via SelectReplacementTile.

SNAP is preferred over REMOVE because the model expressed an intent
to place an enemy at a specific x column; relocating preserves that
intent within a tight tolerance, whereas removing discards it.

The 3-row search depth is a heuristic compromise. A larger search
would risk teleporting enemies across large gaps to ground far below,
which feels unphysical. A smaller search would force more removals
and discard more model intent.

1.4 GROUND CONTINUITY VIA BOTTOM-ROW COMPLETION

The bottom row of every chunk is forced to Solid where it is currently
Empty by BottomRowCompletion. Non-Empty tiles are preserved (Solid,
Breakable, Enemy, Coin, pipe body, bullet bill body) because the model
may legitimately place those at ground level.

This pass runs FIRST in the iteration order so subsequent support
validations have a stable ground surface to test against.

1.5 PASS ORDERING IN THE ITERATIVE LOOP

The seven-pass loop in ChunkStructureRepair.RepairAll now runs in this
order each iteration:

   (1) BottomRowCompletion
   (2) PipeRepair                    (Iteration 5)
   (3) PipeSupportRepair             (Iteration 6 - new)
   (4) BulletBillRepair              (Iteration 5)
   (5) BulletBillSupportRepair       (Iteration 6 - new)
   (6) EnemyRepair                   (Iteration 6 - new)
   (7) QuestionBlockRepair           (Iteration 5)

BottomRowCompletion runs first so subsequent support checks see the
final ground state. PipeRepair runs before PipeSupportRepair so the
support check only encounters complete pipes (the structure-validity
pass cleans up loose pieces first). BulletBill the same. EnemyRepair
runs after structures are finalized so the snap-to-ground search does
not target a tile that is about to be cleared. QuestionBlockRepair
runs last because its adjacency rules depend on which pipes and
bullet bills survived support validation.

The loop iterates because some repairs cascade. Examples:

   Extending a pipe body downward may push it through a row that
   used to contain a misplaced QuestionFull, which is now invalid.

   Removing a floating bullet bill may free the cell immediately
   below it, enabling a previously-blocked enemy SNAP to succeed.

   BottomRowCompletion may turn a previously-impossible pipe
   extension into a possible one by establishing ground at the
   bottom row that the extension path can now terminate against.

Typical chunks converge in 2 to 3 iterations.

PART 2: IMPLEMENTATION - FOUR NEW REPAIR CLASSES
================================================================================

2.1 ASSEMBLY LOCATION

All new classes live in the assembly c2-pcg.flow-matching-eval.

2.2 BottomRowCompletion.cs (36 lines)

Single-pass repair. For each x in [0, chunk.Width): if
chunk[x, chunk.Height-1] is Empty, set it to Solid; otherwise preserve
the existing tile. Returns true if any change was made.

2.3 PipeSupportRepair.cs (226 lines)

Anchored on each PipeTopLeft that forms a complete 2x2 pipe
(verified by checking the three companion cells). For each such
anchor:

   FindPipeBottomBodyRow walks downward through PipeBodyLeft+Right
   pairs to locate the bottom of the column.

   IsPipeBottomSupported checks that (x, bottomY+1) and (x+1, bottomY+1)
   are Solid or Breakable, or that bottomY equals chunk.Height-1.

   If unsupported, DecideExtendOrRemove returns EXTEND or REMOVE per
   the policy of Section 1.2.

   TryExtendPipeBodyToGround walks downward from bottomY+1, writing
   PipeBodyLeft+Right pairs into Empty cells until ground is reached
   or the chunk bottom. Returns false if blocked by a non-Empty
   non-ground tile.

   RemoveEntirePipeColumn replaces the 2x2 anchor and every body row
   below it with SelectReplacementTile results.

The DecideExtendOrRemove count input is existingSupportedCount, not
the unrestricted complete-pipe count, so the chunk converges toward
[1, 3] SUPPORTED pipes rather than [1, 3] complete-but-possibly-
floating pipes.

2.4 BulletBillSupportRepair.cs (167 lines)

Mirror of PipeSupportRepair for 1-tile-wide structures. Same
EXTEND-vs-REMOVE policy, same SelectReplacementTile fallback. The
only structural difference is that the bullet bill column is 1 tile
wide so all the 2x2 anchor and body-row sweeps reduce to single-tile
checks.

2.5 EnemyRepair.cs (133 lines)

Single-pass repair. For each Enemy tile:

   IsStandingOnGround checks (x, y+1) for Solid or Breakable.

   FindSnapTargetRow searches downward up to SearchDepth = 3 rows
   for a Solid or Breakable tile. Returns the row immediately above
   the ground tile, or -1 if no ground is found in range or the
   path is blocked.

   If FindSnapTargetRow returns a valid row: write Enemy at that
   row and clear the original row via SelectReplacementTile.

   Otherwise: clear the original row via SelectReplacementTile.

3.1 ORCHESTRATOR CHANGES

ChunkStructureRepair.RepairAll now invokes seven passes per iteration
instead of three:

   BottomRowCompletion.RepairOnce(repaired)
   PipeRepair.RepairOnce(repaired, config, rng)
   PipeSupportRepair.RepairOnce(repaired, config, rng)
   BulletBillRepair.RepairOnce(repaired, config, rng)
   BulletBillSupportRepair.RepairOnce(repaired, config, rng)
   EnemyRepair.RepairOnce(repaired)
   QuestionBlockRepair.RepairOnce(repaired)

The convergence-or-MaxIterations loop is unchanged.

3.2 GENERATE ENTRYPOINT CHANGES

None. The generate entrypoint at PcgFlowMatchingGenerateEntryPoint.cs
still calls ChunkStructureRepair.RepairAll exactly once per chunk.
All Iteration 6 changes are internal to the orchestrator and the new
repair classes. The --no-repair flag continues to work as in
Iteration 5: when set, the seven new passes do not run and the
post-repair counts equal the pre-repair counts.

PART 6: LIMITATIONS AND HONEST DISCUSSION FOR THESIS
================================================================================

6.1 STRUCTURAL CHANGES TO MODEL OUTPUT

Iteration 6 is more aggressive than Iteration 5 in modifying the
model output. BottomRowCompletion writes Solid into Empty cells that
the model explicitly predicted; PipeSupportRepair may add many rows
of pipe body that the model did not predict; EnemyRepair may move an
enemy to a position the model did not predict.

The thesis should be honest about this aggressiveness:

   "The repair pipeline modifies generated chunks more substantially
    than a typical post-processing pass would. Each modification is
    motivated by a hard structural rule (no floating structures, no
    discontinuous ground, no floating enemies) and is implemented by
    a deterministic operation that respects the model's predicted
    tile positions where possible. The hybrid nature - learned soft
    constraints plus rule-based hard constraints - is a deliberate
    design choice consistent with production game procedural
    generation pipelines."

A reviewer may ask whether the repair changes the chunks so much
that the model contribution is diluted. The defensible answer is:
the model contribution is the tile-type distribution and the
spatial pattern at the chunk level (where pipes appear, where
question blocks cluster, where bullet bills are placed); the repair
enforces the structural-integrity invariants that the per-tile MSE
loss provably cannot. The thesis comparison should be: chunks
generated WITHOUT any structural model (random tile placement)
versus chunks generated WITH the CFM model and the repair pipeline.
The pattern coherence (biome distinctness, plausible chunk shapes,
positional sensibility) is the model's contribution.

6.2 LIMITATIONS OF THE SUPPORT VALIDATION

   The 3-row enemy search depth is a heuristic. Enemies at the top
   of the chunk far from ground will always be removed. A different
   pipeline could try to extend the column downward by inserting
   ground tiles below the enemy; we do not do this because it would
   conflict with the model's predicted geometry above the enemy.

   PipeSupportRepair extends body indefinitely until ground or chunk
   bottom is reached. A chunk where the model placed a pipe top at
   row 1 with no ground until row 13 will get an extremely tall
   pipe. This is visually unusual but not invalid per SMB rules
   (some real SMB pipes are quite tall).

   The implicit-ground convention at the chunk bottom assumes the
   chunk is rendered with a ground tile immediately below the last
   row. This is consistent with how chunks are stitched into longer
   levels but may not be appropriate for chunks rendered in
   isolation.

6.3 EXPECTED VIOLATION CEILING

After Iteration 6 the expected violation count per chunk is at most
the count of tiles the failure-mode analyzer recognizes as broken
WITHIN the repaired output. Since all six categories the analyzer
checks are now enforced (BrokenPipeHorizontal, BrokenPipeTopLeft,
BrokenPipeTopRight, BrokenBulletBill, FloatingEnemy,
DiscontinuousGround), the expected post-repair violation count is
near zero. Realistic numbers:

   0 BrokenPipe* violations - PipeRepair clears them.
   0 BrokenBulletBill violations - BulletBillRepair clears them.
   0 DiscontinuousGround violations - BottomRowCompletion clears them.
   0 to 1 FloatingEnemy violations - SNAP may produce a position
      whose ground was REMOVE'd in the same iteration, leaving the
      enemy on top of a Empty tile until the next iteration catches
      it. Usually fixed within 2 iterations.

The remaining violations after Iteration 6 should be on the order of
0 to 5 per chunk, achieved primarily through structural enforcement
rather than model improvement.
