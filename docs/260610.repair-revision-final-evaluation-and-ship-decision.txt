PART 5: RESIDUAL TREETOP VIOLATIONS ARE INTENTIONAL
================================================================================

The Treetop post-repair count of 65 violations consists entirely of
DiscontinuousGround events. These are NOT failures of the repair
pipeline. They are the gaps in the ground row that
BottomRowCompletion deliberately preserves for Treetop chunks. The
biome is defined in real SMB by exactly these gaps; forcing
continuous ground for Treetop produced the Iteration 7 visual
failure that this report's lineage of revisions resolved.

5.1 WHY THE ANALYZER COUNTS THEM AS VIOLATIONS

FailureModeAnalyzer.CheckDiscontinuousGround does not know about
biomes. It scans every Empty tile in the bottom row and reports it
as a violation. For Overworld and Underground, this is correct.
For Treetop, the gaps are intentional and the report should be
biome-aware.

5.2 IMPLICATION FOR THESIS NUMBERS

The thesis writeup should report the violation count per biome
separately and explicitly call out the DiscontinuousGround for
Treetop as a known biome feature. A footnote in Chapter 4 should
read approximately:

   "Treetop chunks retain BottomRowCompletion-skipped ground gaps
    as a biome feature consistent with the source SMB Treetop
    levels (3-3, 5-3). The DiscontinuousGround violations counted
    by FailureModeAnalyzer for Treetop are therefore not strictly
    failures of the model or the repair pipeline; they reflect the
    biome's design. A biome-aware violation analyzer would
    subtract this category for Treetop."

5.3 COMPUTING "ACTUAL" TREETOP VIOLATIONS

If the user wishes to report a single combined number, the
appropriate adjustment is to subtract the DiscontinuousGround
count from the Treetop total:

   Treetop adjusted post-repair total = 65 - 65 = 0

This places Treetop in line with Overworld and Underground at
zero violations per the spirit of the failure-mode analysis.

7.2 THESIS NARRATIVE FOR CHAPTER 4

The chapter structure that this work supports:

   Section 4.1 - Iteration 1: CFM baseline (Iter 1).
      Established that the model converges; baseline 7.7 violations
      per chunk.

   Section 4.2 - Iteration 2: class-balanced and biome conditioning
      experiments (Iter 2 Exp B, B-v2, D).
      Explored rare-tile recall vs. structural fidelity trade-offs.
      Exp D added biome conditioning successfully.

   Section 4.3 - Iteration 3: failed scaling experiment (BC=128 +
      100k steps).
      Negative result with mechanism analysis. Cited from
      docs/260609.

   Section 4.4 - Iteration 4: uniform loss + biome conditioning.
      Reverted class-balanced loss; restored structural fidelity
      while keeping biome distinctness from Exp D.

   Section 4.5 - Iteration 5: chunk structure repair pipeline.
      Hybrid system: the CFM model learns soft structural
      constraints from data; a deterministic repair pipeline
      enforces hard structural constraints (no broken pipes,
      coherent ground, supported structures, accessible question
      blocks, clear launcher paths, biome-aware ground rules).
      The pipeline reduces violation count from ~155 per 10
      chunks to 0 for Overworld and Underground; Treetop retains
      biome-intentional ground gaps.

      The cited reports for Section 4.5 cover the 11 documented
      revisions of the pipeline (Iterations 5 through 10 plus the
      pipe height + Q-block + 14-tile repair revision plus this
      final evaluation), each with a rationale, a code change,
      and a logic-test set. The full 58-test logic suite passes.

   Section 4.6 - Limitations.
      The thesis pipeline has a hard cap on pipe and bullet bill
      heights. Larger or non-canonical structures (e.g. very tall
      pipes that require external climbing scenery) are not
      handled. The Treetop DiscontinuousGround analyzer behavior
      is a known biome-coupling gap; a biome-aware analyzer would
      address it.

   Section 4.7 - Future Work.
      Mario AI Framework integration (% beatable metric, planned
      for 2026-06-15 to 2026-07-12 window between rascunho and
      final). Generation-path integration tests. Discrete diffusion
      model comparison.
