PART 1: REMAINING QUALITATIVE GAPS IN EXPERIMENT D OUTPUTS
================================================================================

Per-chunk inspection of the 30 Experiment D chunks surfaces the
following gaps. The gaps are described as observations, then mapped to
the specific Improvement in later parts that proposes to address them.

1.1 ALL ENEMIES RENDER AS GOOMBAS

    The VGLC encoding uses a single character "E" for all enemies
    (Goomba, Koopa Troopa, Lakitu, Spiny, etc.) so the model itself
    cannot distinguish enemy varieties. The CURRENT renderer chose
    one specific 16-pixel sprite (the canonical Goomba from
    mario-1-1 row 12 col 21) to represent every E tile.

    Result: every generated chunk that contains enemies shows
    visually identical Goombas. The original NES Mario levels have
    visible enemy variety even when VGLC reports them as the same
    "E" character.

    Addressable by: Improvement A (randomized enemy sprite
    rendering). The fix is purely cosmetic - the model output is
    unchanged; only the RENDERING step picks among multiple sprite
    options.

1.2 TREETOP CHUNKS OFTEN HAVE A CONTINUOUS GROUND ROW

    The canonical Mario treetop levels (1-3, 3-3, 5-3, 6-3) feature
    long stretches with NO continuous ground row at the bottom: the
    player is on floating platforms and falling off means death.

    Observed in the 10 treetop chunks generated by Experiment D:
    approximately 6 of 10 chunks have a continuous Solid ground row
    at the bottom; the other 4 have partial floors with gaps or no
    floor at all. The model has correctly learned the floating-
    platform pattern but has not fully suppressed the "always put
    ground at the bottom" prior that dominates the overworld and
    underground portions of the training set.

    Addressable by: more training (Improvement D) and/or a larger
    model (Improvement C). Both should help but neither will
    eliminate the issue entirely while the dataset proportion is
    9-2-4 (overworld-underground-treetop).

1.3 QUESTION BLOCKS ARE RARE IN OVERWORLD, ABSENT IN TREETOP

    Question block tiles (? and Q) appear in approximately 4 of 10
    overworld chunks and 0 of 10 treetop chunks generated by
    Experiment D. The actual training distribution has question
    blocks more commonly in overworld levels (mario-1-1, 8-1) and
    less commonly in treetop levels (mario-1-3 has a few).

    The model is under-producing question blocks even in biomes
    where they belong. The most likely cause is the sqrt
    inverse-frequency weighting: question blocks have weights ~12
    to 19 (less than the ~30 for bullet bills), so they get less
    gradient attention than is ideal.

    Addressable by: tuning the class-balanced weights specifically
    for question blocks (e.g., manually upweighting beyond the
    sqrt formula). Not a straightforward improvement; not
    prioritized here.

1.4 BULLET BILL CANNONS ARE STILL RARE

    Only 1 of 10 overworld chunks contains a bullet bill cannon
    structure (chunk-003). The training data has bullet bills only
    in 2 levels (mario-5-1 and mario-7-1), so the overworld-biome
    average has bullet bills at very low frequency.

    A sub-biome label such as "overworld-late" (for 7-1 and 8-1)
    vs "overworld-early" (for 1-1, 2-1, etc.) might give the model
    finer-grained control and produce more bullet-bill-rich late-
    game-style chunks on demand.

    Addressable by: sub-biome labels (Improvement out-of-scope for
    TCC; see Part 6).

1.5 SOME FLOATING ENEMIES REMAIN INSIDE BRICK MASSES

    Underground chunks (where brick coverage is dense) occasionally
    have Goombas embedded inside brick areas where Mario could
    never reach them. The biome conditioning improved enemy
    placement on average but did not eliminate this failure mode.

    Addressable by: longer training (Improvement D) and/or larger
    model (Improvement C). Hard to address without a structural
    loss component that explicitly penalizes floating enemies.

1.6 STAIRCASE / END-OF-LEVEL STRUCTURES NOT REPRODUCED

    Real Mario levels frequently end with a 4-tile or 8-tile
    diagonal staircase leading up to the flagpole. None of the 10
    overworld chunks generated by Experiment D contain a clean
    staircase pattern, although some chunks have brick "mountains"
    that loosely resemble one (chunks 003, 005, 010).

    Addressable by: longer training and/or larger model. The
    staircase pattern is rare relative to flat overworld content,
    so the model needs either more capacity or more iterations to
    capture it.

1.7 ANALYZER IS BIOME-AGNOSTIC

    The current FailureModeAnalyzer scores every chunk with the
    same rule set (DiscontinuousGround, BrokenPipe, FloatingEnemy,
    BrokenBulletBill). For treetop chunks specifically,
    DiscontinuousGround is NOT a failure - it is the expected
    pattern. The current analyzer therefore over-penalizes
    structurally-valid treetop chunks.

    For the manuscript Chapter 4 evaluation tables, this matters:
    if you report aggregate FailureMode counts for biome-
    conditional Experiment D, the treetop counts will look bad
    even though the chunks are structurally correct for their
    biome.

    Addressable by: Improvement B (biome-aware analyzer).

PART 2: IMPROVEMENT A - RANDOMIZED ENEMY SPRITE RENDERING
================================================================================

2.1 GAP IT ADDRESSES

    1.1 (all enemies render as Goombas).

2.2 PROPOSED CHANGE

    Add a per-tile-type sprite SELECTION layer in MapPngRenderer.
    Instead of looking up one PNG per TileType in sprites/, support
    looking up MULTIPLE PNGs and randomly selecting one per render.

    Concrete sprite collection for Enemy:
      sprites/Enemy/Goomba.png        (current sprite, retained)
      sprites/Enemy/KoopaTroopa.png   (new, extracted from a level
                                       containing a Koopa)
      sprites/Enemy/Lakitu.png        (optional, may not be present
                                       in the 15 SMB training levels)

    File layout change:
      Before: sprites/Enemy.png
      After:  sprites/Enemy.png         (kept for backward compat)
              sprites/Enemy.alt1.png    (optional alternative sprites)
              sprites/Enemy.alt2.png

    MapPngRenderer change: when loading sprites, look for both
    "<TileTypeName>.png" (primary) and "<TileTypeName>.alt*.png"
    (alternatives). Per-render-tile, pick uniformly at random.

    Extraction script change: extract-sprites.py grows to optionally
    extract a Koopa Troopa sprite from a level that contains one
    (the SMB training set has Koopas in mario-4-1, mario-5-1,
    mario-6-1 area positions where the player encounters them).

2.3 EFFORT

    Small (estimated 1 hour total).
      30 min: identify Koopa Troopa pixel positions in source PNGs.
      15 min: extend extract-sprites.py to support alt sprites.
      15 min: extend MapPngRenderer to load alt sprites and pick at
              random per blit.

2.4 IMPACT

    Small for the model (no change to learning, no change to
    Failure analyzer counts). Medium for the visual evaluation
    chunks shown in the manuscript Chapter 4 figures: enemy
    variety makes the generated chunks "look more like real Mario
    levels" qualitatively.

2.5 SCOPE

    Optional. Recommended if time permits in the remaining 14
    days before the manuscript deadline.

PART 3: IMPROVEMENT B - BIOME-AWARE FailureModeAnalyzer
================================================================================

3.1 GAP IT ADDRESSES

    1.7 (analyzer is biome-agnostic).

3.2 PROPOSED CHANGE

    Replace FailureModeAnalyzer.Analyze(map) with a per-biome
    variant FailureModeAnalyzer.AnalyzeForBiome(map, biome). The
    rules become biome-conditional:

      Overworld:
        DiscontinuousGround   counts as before
        BrokenPipe            counts as before
        FloatingEnemy         counts as before
        BrokenBulletBill      counts as before
        NEW MissingCaveCeiling   does NOT count (caves not expected)

      Underground:
        DiscontinuousGround   counts as before
        FloatingEnemy         counts as before
        NEW MissingCaveCeiling   COUNTS (cave expected) - new failure mode
        BrokenPipe            counts as before
        BrokenBulletBill      does NOT count (cannons very rare
                              in underground training data)

      Treetop:
        DiscontinuousGround   does NOT count (gaps are expected)
        FloatingEnemy         counts as before
        BrokenPipe            counts as before (when pipes appear)
        BrokenBulletBill      does NOT count
        NEW IsolatedPlatform     does NOT count (platforms expected)

    Implementation note: the existing FailureModeAnalysisResult
    struct accumulates separate per-rule counts. The biome-aware
    version returns a struct of the same shape, but rules that
    don't apply to the biome return 0 unconditionally.

3.3 EFFORT

    Medium (estimated 3 to 4 hours total).
      1 hour: add biome parameter to AnalyzeForBiome and route
              biome to per-rule guards.
      1 hour: implement MissingCaveCeiling and IsolatedPlatform
              rules (each is a small per-tile check).
      1 hour: update generate entrypoint to call the biome-aware
              variant and report per-biome aggregate counts.
      30 min: update FeatureTestRunner with new test cases.
      30 min: rerun the 30 biome-conditional chunks to produce
              biome-aware violation counts for the manuscript.

3.4 IMPACT

    Medium for the manuscript Chapter 4 evaluation tables. Without
    biome-aware analysis, the treetop chunks score badly on
    DiscontinuousGround (because they have no continuous ground)
    even though the lack of ground is correct per their biome. The
    biome-aware analyzer gives honest per-biome numbers.

3.5 SCOPE

    Optional but strongly recommended. The biome-aware aggregate
    counts give the manuscript a cleaner quantitative evaluation
    story than the current biome-agnostic ones.

PART 4: IMPROVEMENT C - LARGER MODEL (BLOCKED ON CHANNELS FIX)
================================================================================

4.1 GAP IT ADDRESSES

    1.2 (treetop ground prior), 1.5 (floating enemies), 1.6
    (no staircases). All three are model-capacity-limited gaps.

4.2 PROPOSED CHANGE

    Bump BaseChannels from 64 to 128 (or higher) AFTER implementing
    the architectural initialization fix described in
    docs/260603.unetbaseline-channels-scaling-fix-plan.txt. Larger
    capacity should improve representation of rarer patterns such
    as staircases and discontinuous treetop floors.

4.3 EFFORT

    Depends on the channels fix being implemented first.

      Phase 1: implement the channels-fix (Small to Medium effort,
               estimated 1 hour) per the companion plan document.
      Phase 2: change BaseChannels from 64 to 128 in train and
               generate entrypoints (Small, 2 lines).
      Phase 3: retrain at 20000 steps (Small, approximately 20 to
               40 minutes on the local RTX 5060 GPU).
      Phase 4: regenerate per-biome chunks and re-evaluate.

    Total: estimated 2 to 3 hours including verification.

4.4 IMPACT

    Potentially large. A 4x larger parameter count gives the model
    room to represent rarer patterns (staircases, complex pipe
    arrangements, biome-specific spatial structures). The
    underground biome should also benefit: more capacity may
    eliminate the floating-enemy-inside-brick-mass artifact.

4.5 SCOPE

    Recommended if the channels fix is implemented. The two
    improvements together (channels fix + larger model) are the
    likely candidates to push generation quality beyond the
    current Experiment D baseline.

PART 5: IMPROVEMENT D - LONGER TRAINING
================================================================================

5.1 GAP IT ADDRESSES

    1.2 (treetop ground prior), 1.5 (floating enemies), 1.6
    (no staircases). Same gaps as Improvement C but addressed by
    optimization rather than capacity.

5.2 PROPOSED CHANGE

    Bump NumSteps from 20000 to 50000 or 100000 in
    PcgFlowMatchingTrainEntryPoint.cs. The Iteration 1 baseline
    loss-curve analysis (docs/260529.tcc-iteration-1-first-end-to-
    end-result.txt) showed the loss plateaued by step 7500, so
    additional steps will give diminishing returns. However the
    extra optimization may still help rare patterns get more
    gradient signal time.

5.3 EFFORT

    Small (estimated 30 to 45 minutes for 100000 steps on RTX
    5060).

5.4 IMPACT

    Small. Loss-curve analysis says the model is essentially
    converged at 20000 steps. The expected gain is marginal.

5.5 SCOPE

    Optional. Cheap to test; do it before deciding on Improvement
    C if you want to be sure model capacity is the bottleneck and
    not training duration.

PART 6: OUT-OF-SCOPE IMPROVEMENTS NOTED FOR FUTURE WORK
================================================================================

These improvements are documented here as ideas worth pursuing in a
future paper, but are not recommended for the TCC manuscript timeline.

6.1 SUB-BIOME LABELS

    Replace the 3-class biome label (Overworld / Underground /
    Treetop) with finer-grained labels (overworld-pipe-heavy /
    overworld-bullet-bill / overworld-castle-approach /
    underground-coin-room / underground-pipe-network / ...).
    Would give the user finer control over generated content but
    requires manual re-labeling of the 15 training levels at
    sub-biome granularity, plus retraining.
    Out-of-scope: estimated 1 to 2 days of focused work.

6.2 CLASSIFIER-FREE GUIDANCE

    Train with biome-dropout (e.g., 10 percent of the time pass
    BiomeTypeEnum.Error as the label, training the model to also
    handle unconditional generation). At inference, use the
    classifier-free guidance trick:
      v_guided = v_unconditional + scale * (v_conditional - v_unconditional)
    This pushes the biome conditioning signal stronger at
    inference time. Likely to improve biome consistency further,
    but not strictly necessary given Experiment D already shows
    strong biome differentiation.
    Out-of-scope: estimated 1 day of focused work.

6.3 DISCRETE FLOW MATCHING

    Replace the continuous CFM objective with a discrete flow
    matching objective (Campbell et al. 2024) designed
    specifically for categorical data. Would replace the per-pixel
    MSE on continuous one-hot velocity with a categorical cross-
    entropy on tile classes. Should produce sharper per-tile
    predictions and potentially eliminate the remaining
    floating-enemy issues.
    Out-of-scope: estimated 2 to 3 days of focused work plus
    paper reading. Mentioned in
    docs/260602.iteration-2-plan.txt Part 4 as Experiment C.

6.4 LONGER GENERATION SEQUENCES

    Currently the generator produces standalone 28-by-14 chunks.
    Real Mario levels are 200+ tiles wide. A future paper could
    add a "stitch chunks together" generation mode that produces
    full-length playable levels by sampling consecutive chunks
    conditioned on the previous chunk's right edge.
    Out-of-scope: requires a different model formulation (e.g.,
    autoregressive or conditional-on-context).

6.5 CONDITIONAL GENERATION ON GAMEPLAY PROPERTIES

    Extend biome conditioning to additional axes such as
    difficulty (easy / medium / hard), enemy density (low / med /
    high), or item availability (sparse / generous). Would
    require manually labeling each chunk along each axis. Promising
    for a follow-up paper but out-of-scope for the TCC.
