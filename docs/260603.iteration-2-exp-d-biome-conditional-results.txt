OBJECTIVE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Iteration 2 Experiment B v2 (sqrt inverse-frequency class-balanced loss,
docs/260603.iteration-2-exp-b-v2-and-biome-averaging-finding.txt)
produced the best unconditional Conditional Flow Matching model of the
three loss variants tried (Iteration 1 unweighted, Exp B v1 raw inverse,
Exp B v2 sqrt inverse). It also exposed a structural limitation that no
loss-shape modification could fix: the unconditional model averages
across the biomes in the training corpus, baking the underground biome's
cave-ceiling pattern (a horizontal Breakable run at the top of the
chunk) into nearly every generated sample regardless of intended biome.

Experiment D adds biome conditioning to the U-Net. Each VGLC level is
assigned a biome label (Overworld, Underground, Treetop), every chunk
inherits the label from its parent level via TileMapChunker, and the
U-Net receives a learnable per-biome embedding that is summed with the
sinusoidal time embedding before injection into each ResidualConvBlock.
At inference, the user requests a specific biome via the --biome
overworld|underground|treetop flag on the generate CLI.

This report documents the result. The hypothesis being tested is that
biome-conditional generation produces visibly different content per
biome, with each biome's content matching the structural properties of
that biome in the training data. Specifically:
  - Overworld generations should have NO cave ceiling and should be
    sky-dominant with floating brick platforms and pipes.
  - Underground generations should have a Breakable cave ceiling on
    every chunk.
  - Treetop generations should have elevated brick platforms with
    sky around them and few or no full pipes.

    LearningRate     5e-5

    NumSteps         20000
    BaseChannels     64
    TimeEmbeddingDim 128
    NumBiomes        4
    Seed             42
    Gradient clip    max_norm = 0.5

The class-balanced sqrt-inverse-frequency weighting from Experiment B v2
is retained. The only architectural change vs B v2 is the addition of
the biome embedding lookup table (4 by 128 = 512 new parameters out of
approximately 3 million total) and its summation into the time
embedding within Forward.

Loss trajectory was nearly identical to Experiment B v2: step 0 = 1.035
(vs B v2 1.033), final values in the 0.06 to 0.08 range (vs B v2 0.06
to 0.10). The biome embedding addition did not destabilize training and
did not change the optimization difficulty.

PART 2: PER-BIOME CHUNK ANALYSIS
================================================================================

The 10 chunks generated for each of the 3 biomes were inspected at 2x
upscale (672 by 336 pixels). Patterns observed within each biome are
nearly uniform across the 10 samples, so each biome is described as a
single profile with notable per-chunk variations called out.

2.1 OVERWORLD (10 chunks, generated-png-overworld/)

    UNIFORM PROPERTIES (all 10 chunks):
      - No Breakable tiles in the upper 3 rows. The top of every chunk
        is pure Empty (sky blue).
      - Continuous Solid (ground) row at the bottom, zero gaps.
      - Multiple green Pipe structures, several of which are
        structurally valid 2-wide 2-tall pipes with PipeTopLeft +
        PipeTopRight on top and PipeBodyLeft + PipeBodyRight below.
      - Goombas placed correctly: either standing on Solid ground at
        the bottom row, or standing on top of a Pipe.
      - Floating brick platforms (short Breakable runs of 3 to 8
        tiles) at mid-screen heights, not touching the top.

    NOTABLE PER-CHUNK FEATURES:
      Chunk 002:  Goombas standing ON TOP of pipes (the canonical
                  Mario 1-1 enemy placement). Multiple pipes of
                  varying heights.
      Chunk 003:  Bullet Bill cannon (B + b stacked) visible at lower
                  left, on the ground. Adjacent brick "mountain"
                  structure on the right.
      Chunk 005:  Three pipes of varying heights plus a large brick
                  "castle" structure on the right that resembles the
                  end-of-level fortress.
      Chunk 010:  Densest with pipes (5 pipes) plus a brick mountain
                  on the right.

    INTERPRETATION:
      The overworld generations look like sections of a real Super
      Mario Bros 1-1, 5-1, or 8-1 overworld level. The model has
      learned that overworld biome means: sky-dominant top, ground at
      bottom, pipes are the primary non-ground vertical feature, brick
      platforms float at mid-height, enemies sit on ground or pipes.

2.2 UNDERGROUND (10 chunks, generated-png-underground/)

    UNIFORM PROPERTIES (all 10 chunks):
      - Continuous Breakable cave ceiling spanning the top 1 to 3
        rows. EVERY chunk has this. None of the overworld or treetop
        chunks have it.
      - Brick "stalactites" hanging from the ceiling into the lower
        cave interior, of varying lengths.
      - Coins scattered at floor level and inside the cave interior
        (consistent with Mario 1-2 / 4-2 coin-room patterns).
      - Continuous Solid ground row at the bottom.
      - Sparse pipes (few chunks have any) and few question blocks
        (mario-1-2 and mario-4-2 also have few of these in the
        training data).
      - Goombas at ground level, some inside the cave interior.

    NOTABLE PER-CHUNK FEATURES:
      Chunk 001:  Cave ceiling with 3 question blocks embedded near
                  the upper-left side. Stalactite reaching down to
                  mid-screen.
      Chunk 003:  Pipes on the lower platform (one of the few
                  underground chunks with pipes).
      Chunk 005:  Pipes integrated with a brick "platform" extending
                  out from the cave wall.
      Chunk 010:  Cave with a row of pipes at the bottom; lower
                  ceiling coverage than other chunks (about 60 percent
                  of the top row).

    INTERPRETATION:
      The underground generations look like sections of Mario 1-2 or
      Mario 4-2. The model has learned that underground biome means:
      Breakable ceiling, brick walls and stalactites, coins inside
      the cave, ground at the bottom, sparse pipes and question
      blocks.

2.3 TREETOP (10 chunks, generated-png-treetop/)

    UNIFORM PROPERTIES (all 10 chunks):
      - No Breakable cave ceiling. The top of every chunk is pure
        Empty (sky blue).
      - Floating brick platforms at multiple heights. The platforms
        are SHORT (3 to 8 tiles wide) and ELEVATED.
      - Many coins (more per chunk than overworld or underground)
        arranged in horizontal lines just above the platforms.
        This is the canonical "coin trail above platform" Mario
        treetop pattern.
      - NO green pipes in 9 of 10 chunks; chunk 008 has a
        single short pipe fragment. The training-data treetop
        levels (mario-1-3, 3-3, 5-3, 6-3) have minimal pipe content.
      - NO question blocks in 8 of 10 chunks.
      - NO bullet bills in any chunk.
      - Bottom row is MIXED: some chunks have a continuous Solid
        ground row, others have a partial floor with gaps, others
        have only floating platforms with NO bottom ground at all
        (matching the canonical "tree platforms over a void"
        treetop layout).
      - Goombas standing on platforms or at floor level when ground
        is present.

    NOTABLE PER-CHUNK FEATURES:
      Chunk 002:  Long horizontal coin line of approximately 8 coins
                  above a brick platform.
      Chunk 005:  Multiple elevated platforms with coin lines above
                  each.
      Chunk 010:  Coin trail spanning most of the chunk width with
                  Goombas on the platform below.

    INTERPRETATION:
      The treetop generations look like sections of Mario 1-3 or
      Mario 5-3. The model has learned that treetop biome means:
      sky-dominant, floating short brick platforms, coin trails above
      platforms, no cave structures, no pipes, often no continuous
      ground.

PART 3: HYPOTHESIS VERIFICATION
================================================================================

The three hypotheses stated in the Objective are evaluated against the
10-chunk samples:

3.1 HYPOTHESIS: Overworld generations should have NO cave ceiling
    and should be sky-dominant with floating brick platforms and pipes.

    RESULT: CONFIRMED. 10 of 10 overworld chunks have no Breakable
    tile in the upper 3 rows. 10 of 10 have multiple pipes. Sky
    coverage is approximately 50 to 65 percent of each chunk.

3.2 HYPOTHESIS: Underground generations should have a Breakable cave
    ceiling on every chunk.

    RESULT: CONFIRMED. 10 of 10 underground chunks have a horizontal
    Breakable run in the upper rows. 8 of 10 have ceiling coverage
    above 70 percent of the chunk width.

3.3 HYPOTHESIS: Treetop generations should have elevated brick
    platforms with sky around them and few or no full pipes.

    RESULT: CONFIRMED. 10 of 10 treetop chunks have floating
    elevated platforms with sky on multiple sides. 9 of 10 have
    zero pipes (the 10th has one pipe fragment).

All three hypotheses are met. Biome conditioning works.

PART 5: DATASET SCOPE - WHAT VGLC CAN AND CANNOT REPRESENT
================================================================================

The VGLC Super Mario Bros corpus encodes 13 structural tile types
(per smb.json): Empty, Solid, Breakable, QuestionFull, QuestionEmpty,
Enemy, PipeTopLeft, PipeTopRight, PipeBodyLeft, PipeBodyRight, Coin,
BulletBillLauncher, BulletBillBody.

This encoding is deliberately lossy. The original NES Mario level
graphics contain many visual elements that are NOT representable in
the 13-character vocabulary, because the VGLC researchers
(Summerville et al. 2016) chose to encode at the level of
"gameplay-relevant structure" rather than at the level of pixel
appearance.

Elements present in the original PNGs but NOT in the VGLC encoding:

    Visual element                Why not encoded
    ----------------------------  ----------------------------------
    Clouds                        purely decorative, no gameplay
    Bushes, hills (decorative)    purely decorative
    Castle, flagpole              level-end fixtures, not tiles
    Mushroom powerup              emerges from a Question block at
                                  runtime; not a tile in the static
                                  level data
    Fire Flower powerup           same as Mushroom
    Star powerup                  same
    Piranha Plant                 emerges from a pipe at runtime
    Bullet (projectile)           fired by a BulletBill cannon at
                                  runtime; not a tile
    Distinct enemy varieties      Koopa Troopa (turtle), Bullet Bill
                                  (in flight), Lakitu, Spiny, etc.
                                  are all collapsed into the single
                                  Enemy character "E" in VGLC
    Distinct solid-block types    The indestructible 3D cube block is
                                  encoded as the same character X as
                                  the flat ground floor; no
                                  visual distinction in VGLC
    Vine / beanstalk              emerges from a special Question
                                  block at runtime
    Spring trampoline             rare object, not encoded

Implication for the generated chunks: our model can NEVER produce
clouds, mushrooms, piranha plants, distinct enemy varieties, or
bullet projectiles. These are not failures of the model; they are
absent from the training vocabulary the model learned. A pixel-
perfect-fidelity model would require a different dataset (e.g., one
that classifies every 16 by 16 NES tile into approximately 200
distinct visual classes instead of 13 structural classes). That is
out of scope for this TCC.

Implication for the manuscript: Chapter 4 should explicitly bound
the claim to "the model produces structurally-valid Mario-like
chunks within the VGLC structural vocabulary," citing
Summerville et al. 2016 for the encoding convention.

PART 6: CONCLUSION - ITERATION 2 CLOSED
================================================================================

Iteration 2 set out to address four failure modes observed in
Iteration 1 (DiscontinuousGround, BrokenPipe, FloatingEnemy,
fragmented BulletBill cannons). Iteration 2 explored four experiments
of which three were executed:

  Experiment A (LARGER MODEL CAPACITY): BLOCKED on an architectural
  initialization-stability issue when scaling BaseChannels beyond 64.
  Documented in docs/260602.iteration-2-plan.txt Part 2 and in
  docs/260603.unetbaseline-channels-scaling-fix-plan.txt (this
  report's companion document). Fix proposed, deferred to
  Iteration 3.

  Experiment B (CLASS-BALANCED LOSS): partial success. Two variants
  run.
    v1 (raw inverse-frequency): rare-class spam dominates; documented
       as a failure mode.
    v2 (sqrt inverse-frequency): best of the unconditional models;
       resolves ground discontinuity; surfaces the biome-averaging
       structural limitation.

  Experiment D (CONDITIONAL GENERATION): SUCCESS. The final
  Iteration 2 configuration combines sqrt inverse-frequency class
  weighting with learnable biome embeddings summed into the time
  embedding. Produces biome-clean per-biome generations matching the
  structural properties of each training biome.

  Experiment C (DISCRETE FLOW MATCHING): not executed. Not needed
  given the success of Experiment D for the TCC scope.

Final configuration:
  unet-conditional-checkpoint.bin trained with sqrt inverse-frequency
  class weights + biome embedding conditioning. This is the model
  that will produce all generated samples for the TCC Chapter 4
  results section. The user invokes per-biome generation via:

    dotnet run --project c4-cmd.pcg-flow-matching-generate
      -- unet-conditional-checkpoint.bin --biome overworld

  Replace "overworld" with "underground" or "treetop" to target
  other biomes.

Ablation evidence preserved:

  unet-baseline-checkpoint.bin           Iteration 1 unweighted
  unet-class-balanced-checkpoint.bin     Exp B v1 raw inverse
  unet-sqrt-balanced-checkpoint.bin      Exp B v2 sqrt inverse
  unet-conditional-checkpoint.bin        Exp D sqrt + biome
                                         (FINAL CONFIGURATION)

The four checkpoints together form the iterative research narrative
for Chapter 4: we identified a problem, we tried a fix, the fix had
its own problem, we found a deeper cause, we addressed that cause, we
got biome-clean generation. Each step is supported by its own
ablation checkpoint and its own per-chunk analysis report.
