PART 1: HYPOTHESIS AND ITERATION 3 SETUP
================================================================================

1.1 HYPOTHESIS BEING TESTED

Iteration 2 Experiment D (biome-conditional UnetBaseline with BaseChannels=64
and NumSteps=20000) produced chunks that are visually recognizable as Super
Mario Bros level segments with biome-distinct features. However, the
FailureModeAnalyzer in c2-pcg.flow-matching-eval reports a non-zero violation
count in every sample. The hypothesis driving Iteration 3 was that two
changes would jointly reduce structural violations:

   (H1) Increasing BaseChannels from 64 to 128 quadruples the UnetBaseline
        internal feature dimension and approximately quadruples the model's
        parameter count. With more capacity, the model should fit the
        training distribution more faithfully and reduce structural failure
        modes such as discontinuous ground and floating tiles.

   (H2) Increasing NumSteps from 20000 to 100000 gives the model five times
        more optimization steps to find a deeper minimum of the weighted
        CFM loss surface.

A subsidiary change was required to make (H1) feasible. An initial attempt
to run at BaseChannels=128 with the standard ResidualConvBlock
initialization diverged to infinity at training step approximately 50,
because the unnormalized residual shortcut compounds variance through six
residual blocks at the larger feature width. The fix added zero-initialization
of SecondConv weights and bias inside ResidualConvBlock.cs lines 48 to 55,
so that each residual block computes "result = 0 + shortcut = shortcut" at
initialization. This pattern is canonical in DDPM (Ho et al. 2020), Stable
Diffusion (Rombach et al. 2022), and FLUX.

PART 4: DIAGNOSIS - THREE INDEPENDENT FAILURE MECHANISMS
================================================================================

The regression cannot be attributed to a single cause. Three mechanisms
operate together; removing any one would partially recover quality but
not fully restore Experiment D behavior.

4.1 MECHANISM 1: CLASS-BALANCED LOSS TIMES EXTRA CAPACITY EQUALS
                  RARE-CLASS OVEREMPHASIS

The sqrt-inverse class weights computed at training start by
TileTypeFrequencyComputer.BuildSqrtInverseFrequencyWeightTensor are:

   Tile type            Count    Weight   Effective LR multiplier
   -------------------- -------- -------- -----------------------
   Empty                 850570    0.575                  0.6 times
   Solid                  83322    1.836                  1.8 times
   Breakable              23529    3.455                  3.5 times
   QuestionFull             768   19.121                 19.1 times
   QuestionEmpty           1788   12.532                 12.5 times
   Enemy                   7265    6.217                  6.2 times
   PipeTopLeft             2310   11.025                 11.0 times
   PipeTopRight            2309   11.028                 11.0 times
   PipeBodyLeft            4966    7.520                  7.5 times
   PipeBodyRight           4963    7.522                  7.5 times
   Coin                    4502    7.898                  7.9 times
   BulletBillLauncher       421   25.826                 25.8 times
   BulletBillBody           343   28.612                 28.6 times

At BaseChannels=64 the UnetBaseline had limited capacity. Most of that
capacity was committed to learning the bulk-distribution structure
(Empty + Solid tiles, which together account for approximately 95
percent of training tokens) because the aggregate weighted loss on
those tiles, while individually small per-token, dominated due to
their volume.

At BaseChannels=128 the model has approximately four times more
parameters. The aggregate Empty + Solid loss is still dominant in
expectation, but enough surplus capacity is available that the
rare-class gradients (with their 25.8 times and 28.6 times weight
multipliers) can also drive learning. The model devotes that surplus
capacity to learning rare-class distributions WITHOUT enforcing the
structural constraints that hold them in valid positions in real
Mario levels.

Concrete manifestation: BulletBillLauncher appears in approximately
14 percent of training-set chunks. In Iteration 3 output, it appears
in approximately 80 to 100 percent of generated chunks (visual estimate;
not measured by FailureModeAnalyzer). The model has learned the signal
"BulletBill is important, produce often" without learning the signal
"BulletBill should be on ground level, paired with BulletBillBody
immediately above."

This is the rare-class hallucination failure mode documented in
class-balanced training of generative models (see Cui et al. 2019,
"Class-Balanced Loss Based on Effective Number of Samples"). The
sqrt-inverse moderator was supposed to soften this; the data shows it
was insufficient at BaseChannels=128.

4.2 MECHANISM 2: 100K STEPS PAST CONVERGENCE EQUALS STRUCTURAL EROSION

By step 20000 the loss has converged. The Adam optimizer continues to
compute gradient signals from each batch and apply weight updates, but
these updates carry no learning signal beyond batch-composition noise.

Each weight update applied to a converged network introduces a small
random perturbation. Over 80000 additional steps, the accumulated
perturbation drifts the weights away from the minimum the model reached
at step 20000.

Structural features (continuous ground floor, full pipes, biome-specific
tile selection) are encoded in the UnetBaseline as delicate combinations
of weights in the early Conv2d layers and the biome embedding projection.
Random drift erodes these combinations preferentially because they
involve precise tile-level geometry; in contrast, the rare-tile-
overemphasis behavior is more robust to small weight perturbations
because it depends only on average output statistics.

Net effect over the 80000 unnecessary steps: structural features
degrade, rare-tile spam persists.

4.3 MECHANISM 3: SECONDCONV ZERO-INIT WEAKENED BIOME EMBEDDING
                  PROPAGATION

The forward pass of ResidualConvBlock at ResidualConvBlock.cs:58 to 76
implements the following sequence:

    hidden = FirstConv.forward(x)
    hidden = FirstNorm.forward(hidden)
    hidden = silu(hidden)
    timeProjected = TimeProjection.forward(timeEmbedding)
    hidden = hidden + timeProjected.unsqueeze(-1).unsqueeze(-1)
    hidden = SecondConv.forward(hidden)        <-- WEIGHTS ZEROED
    hidden = SecondNorm.forward(hidden)
    hidden = silu(hidden)
    shortcut = Shortcut.forward(x)
    result = hidden + shortcut

In the Iteration 3 zero-init configuration, SecondConv.weight starts
at zero. Therefore at step 0, "hidden" after SecondConv.forward is
identically zero regardless of input. The residual block computes
"result = 0 + shortcut = shortcut" for the first thousands of training
steps until SecondConv weights drift away from zero.

The biome embedding flows through TimeProjection and is added to
"hidden" before SecondConv. When SecondConv.weight is zero, the biome
signal contained in "hidden" is multiplied by zero and lost. The biome
signal in early training has no path through the residual block to
influence the output.

For the UnetBaseline to learn biome conditioning, SecondConv must first
learn to be non-zero AND learn to propagate the specific biome features.
With three biomes (Overworld, Underground, Treetop) splitting capacity,
this is a harder learning problem than at BC=64 where zero-init was not
used and biome features could propagate freely from step 0.

This is the architectural cost of the zero-init pattern: it trades
stability at extreme capacity for slower onset of conditional signal
propagation. DDPM and FLUX papers acknowledge this and compensate with
longer training schedules and learning-rate warmup tricks not present
in the current Iteration 3 training loop.

PART 5: FUNDAMENTAL LIMITS ON ZERO-VIOLATION CHUNKS
================================================================================

The motivating question for this report was whether the pipeline can
ever produce a generated chunk with zero structural violations. This
part argues that the current CFM-plus-argmax formulation has a
non-zero violation floor even under perfect training, and that this
limit is a property of the formulation, not of the training process.

5.1 FIVE SOURCES OF IRREDUCIBLE VIOLATION RATE

   (I1) ARGMAX QUANTIZATION NOISE.
        The UnetBaseline outputs a 14-dimensional velocity field per
        tile cell. The Euler ODE solver in c2-pcg.flow-matching-inference
        integrates this field to a 14-dimensional position vector at
        terminal time, then argmax converts the vector to a discrete
        tile type. Near argmax boundaries where two tile types have
        similar predicted probability, small floating-point noise can
        flip the tile assignment. This produces single-tile anomalies
        (an isolated brick where Empty was intended) that
        FailureModeAnalyzer counts as violations.

   (I2) PER-TOKEN LOSS HAS NO STRUCTURAL TERM.
        CfmLossComputer.ComputeWeightedLoss measures per-tile squared
        velocity error. There is no loss component that penalizes
        the pattern "discontinuous ground" or the pattern "floating
        tile". The model only learns structure indirectly via the
        per-tile distribution. A model that perfectly matches the
        per-tile distribution can still produce structurally invalid
        chunks because the loss does not see chunks as wholes.

   (I3) FINITE TRAINING DATA (2518 CHUNKS).
        At batch size 32 with replacement sampling, after the model
        has seen each chunk approximately 1270 times across 100000
        steps, additional training does not introduce new structural
        patterns. The 2518 chunks constrain the model's structural
        knowledge to the patterns they instantiate. Some structural
        failure modes in generated chunks are simply outside the
        empirical distribution the model could learn.

   (I4) STOCHASTIC EULER INTEGRATION.
        The Euler ODE solver integrates the velocity field with
        finite NFE (number of function evaluations) steps, typically
        10 to 20. At low NFE the solver introduces integration error
        that accumulates as deviation from the true generative
        distribution. Higher NFE reduces this error but not to zero.

   (I5) BIOME LABEL CONFOUNDING.
        The training set has 15 VGLC levels labeled across 4 biomes
        (Error, Overworld, Underground, Treetop), with the Error
        label never used at training time. Some structural patterns
        such as ground continuity are biome-invariant and
        well-supported across all levels. Others such as treetop
        platform arrangement are biome-specific but have few
        training examples. The model averages these signals,
        producing biome-distinct chunks at the population level but
        not perfectly faithful biome reproduction per chunk.

5.2 IMPLICATION FOR THESIS NARRATIVE

Reaching zero violations per chunk with the current
CFM-plus-argmax-plus-FailureModeAnalyzer setup is not achievable. The
thesis should report violation counts COMPARATIVELY (CFM vs random
baseline; CFM at different training durations; CFM with and without
biome conditioning) rather than ABSOLUTELY (CFM achieves N percent
violation-free chunks).

This reframing aligns with the thesis title's framing of generation as a
Constraint Satisfaction Problem: the contribution is showing that CFM
learns constraints from data without explicit specification, not that CFM
achieves perfect satisfaction. Wave Function Collapse achieves zero
violations by construction because it propagates explicitly specified
constraints; CFM achieves an approximation of constraint satisfaction by
learning the distribution of constraint-satisfying examples.

5.3 APPROACHES THAT WOULD REACH ZERO VIOLATIONS (FUTURE WORK)

Approaches that could reach zero violations are outside the scope of
the thesis as currently framed but worth listing for the Future Work
section:

   (Z1) Post-generation heuristic repair: deterministic rules that
        fix bottom-row gaps, complete partial pipes, snap misplaced
        enemies to nearest valid surface. Tractable in approximately
        one day of implementation.

   (Z2) Constraint-aware sampling: modify the Euler solver to project
        intermediate states onto a constraint manifold (e.g. force
        bottom row to be Solid throughout integration). Research
        level.

   (Z3) Discrete diffusion models with structural priors: replace
        continuous CFM with masked discrete diffusion (D3PM-style),
        adding an "allowed-tile" mask at each step that excludes
        structurally invalid patterns. Research level; weeks of work.

   (Z4) Reinforcement learning with violations as reward: fine-tune
        the trained CFM model with negative violation count as reward
        via PPO or similar. Multi-week project.

   (Z5) Hybrid CFM plus Wave Function Collapse: use CFM to propose
        global layout, then WFC to fill in structurally constrained
        regions. Hybrid that combines learned constraints with
        explicit constraints. Research level.
